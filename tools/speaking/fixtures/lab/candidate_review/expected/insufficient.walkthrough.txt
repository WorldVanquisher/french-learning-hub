outcome: insufficient_support (exit 4); standing of the comparison: observation

1. What is counted. 8 clips were compared in pairs, one from each of 8 speakers. Clips of one speaker share a voice, a microphone and a text, so they are not independent: the review counts SPEAKERS as the unit of evidence (groups). The rule asks for at least 30 speakers and at least 30 paired clips; limits are inclusive.
   clips: 8 < 30 -> fail; speakers: 8 < 30 -> fail; 22 more distinct speakers are needed

3. How stable the size of the difference is.
   point estimate -0.1607; 95% speaker-bootstrap interval [-0.2308, -0.1000] (seed 20261119, 2000 resamples).
   five consecutive seeds give lower ends -0.2308..-0.2245 and upper ends -0.1017..-0.0980: the random draw moves an end by at most 0.0063. The weakness is not the draw: every resample can only reuse these 8 speakers.
   with 8 speakers there are 6,435 distinct resamples; with 30 there would be 5.9e+16. With few groups a percentile bootstrap tends to cover less often than its stated 95% (a known property of the method, not measured here).
   leaving one speaker out moves the difference between -0.1837 and -0.1400; speaker s05 moves it most. With 8 speakers each is roughly 12.5% of the evidence (weighted by its words); with 30, roughly 3.3%.

4. Who the speakers are. The review cannot see it; the data manifest and the experiment's own limits say it.
   (no data manifest given: the speakers each split can offer are not counted)
   Whatever the count, the rule is a minimum, not a sufficiency: thirty speakers of read speech still say little about learners, accents or phone microphones. The experiment's limits of evidence name its population.

   pass: 6 (quality.max_wer_difference, quality.max_interval_upper, quality.max_cer_difference, quality.max_group_wer_increase, resources.max_real_time_factor, resources.max_peak_rss_mb)

The limit is not lowered to fit the data; a threshold change is an owner decision in a thresholds file, and the review names the file it used.
